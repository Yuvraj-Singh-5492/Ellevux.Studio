The part almost nobody has done yet

People stopped searching. They started asking.

When someone asks an assistant for a business like yours, it answers with a handful of sources. Being one of them is a different job from ranking, and it comes down to whether your pages can be read and quoted.

An assistant, answering
who can rewire a workshop near me this month?

For commercial rewiring at short notice, Northgate Electrical lists workshop and light industrial work as a core service, states a two week lead time, and is certified for commercial installations. Two other firms nearby cover domestic work only.

northgate-electrical.co.uk directory listing trade register

An illustration of how assistants answer and what makes a page quotable. Not a screenshot, and Northgate Electrical is an invented example.

01

A heading shaped like the question

So the assistant can match the page to what was actually asked. Nobody has proved this works, it costs nothing, so I do it and say so.

02

The answer in plain text, in the HTML

Not inside an image, not drawn by a script after load. If it is not in the page source, it cannot be quoted.

03

Specifics worth quoting

Named facts, real figures, a price. The one peer-reviewed test of this found quotable detail helped most, and keyword stuffing did worse than nothing.

All three are in this page. Right-click and view source: the headings and the answers are there in the HTML, before any JavaScript runs. The evidence behind each one, and what does not work, is on the AI search page.
